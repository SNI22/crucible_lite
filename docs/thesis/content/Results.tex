\chapter{\rm\bfseries Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter reports the empirical findings across all six gripper
variants (\texttt{finray\_18}, \texttt{finray\_18\_model2},
\texttt{finray\_26}, \texttt{finray\_26\_5deg},
\texttt{parallel\_jaw\_stock}, \texttt{parallel\_jaw\_TPU}) organised
around the primary measurement (contact force $F_z$) at swept command
depths and $\pm 2.5^{\circ}$ tilts about the x and y axes. All values
were $F_z$-bias-corrected using the per-session Franka $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ zero-load
reading and pooled across trials with matching command depths and tilts
unless otherwise noted. Interpretation is deferred to
Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.

Figure~\ref{fig:all-variants-raw} gives a first, uncalibrated view of
the full dataset: stationary contact force for all six variants against
the raw commanded press depth, at zero tilt. The two rigid variants rise
steeply and reach the $-60$\,N safety reflex within $3$--$3.5$\,mm of
first contact; all four finray variants rise more gradually and remain
well clear of the reflex out to $14$--$22$\,mm. The remainder of this
chapter reports statistics and figures on the \emph{calibrated} depth
axis introduced in Section~\ref{sec:dcal}, which removes the raw-axis
offsets visible here --- each variant's curve departs from zero at a
different raw depth, an artefact of touch-off and print geometry rather
than gripper behaviour --- and enables direct cross-variant comparison.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{all_variants_Fz_vs_depth_0deg.png}
\caption[All variants, raw depth, zero tilt]{Stationary $F_z$ versus raw
commanded press depth for all six variants at $0^{\circ}$ tilt (shaded
band: $\pm 1\sigma$ across repeats). Rigid variants (circle, square)
saturate the $-60$\,N safety reflex within a few millimetres; finray
variants (triangle, diamond) rise more gradually and extend far beyond.}
\label{fig:all-variants-raw}
\end{figure}

\section{Print-to-Print Manufacturing Variability}
\label{sec:manufacturing-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:m1-vs-m2} compares two independent prints of the same
\texttt{finray\_18} CAD geometry directly on the calibrated depth axis.
Each zeroed at its own first majority-success cell
(Section~\ref{sec:dcal}), the two prints differed by a $+4.6$\,N mean force
offset (second print stiffer) with RMSE $5.4$\,N over the 10\,mm window;
removing the constant offset left a residual RMSE of $2.9$\,N. This
$\approx 5$\,N offset was adopted as the canonical same-session
print-to-print variability, approximately $15\%$ of the knee peak
force.

Cross-session drift on the \emph{same} print, measured by comparing
\texttt{finray\_18} data collected on separate days approximately three
weeks apart, was approximately $7$--$10$\,N at knee and knee-adjacent
depths. Cross-session drift on the same specimen was therefore larger
than same-session print-to-print variability.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{fr18_m1_vs_m2_aligned0.png}
\caption[Print-to-print force comparison]{Contact force versus
calibrated depth for two independently printed copies of
\texttt{finray\_18}, zero tilt, successful cells only. Error bars: one
standard deviation across repeats.}
\label{fig:m1-vs-m2}
\end{figure}

The two prints' primary force-depth response is otherwise nearly
identical (pairwise shape NMSE $0.41\%$, Table~\ref{tab:shape-nmse}),
and even their secondary x-axis bias shares the same depth-gated
envelope as the rest of this thesis's bias measurements
(Section~\ref{sec:bipolar-bias-results}): largest near the boundary and
relaxing toward zero at depth. The two prints differ only in where that
shared envelope sits relative to zero. \texttt{finray\_18}'s bias
crosses zero (negative at the boundary, briefly positive through the
knee, negative again at depth), while \texttt{finray\_18\_model2}'s
stays negative throughout the full measured range; the gap between the
two ($2$--$3.5$\,N near the boundary, shrinking to $<1$\,N by
$d=+10$\,mm) is itself depth-gated, consistent with a shared underlying
shape offset by a shrinking, print-dependent bias rather than two
qualitatively different behaviours. This offset is a bound on
secondary-axis error --- attributable to some combination of
print-instance manufacturing variation and force-estimator bias
(Section~\ref{sec:manufacturing-mechanism}) --- not evidence that the
two prints respond differently to tilt.

\section{Working Range at Zero Tilt}
\label{sec:working-range-zero}

On the calibrated depth axis (Section~\ref{sec:dcal}), at $0^{\circ}$
tilt the parallel-jaw variants exhibited closed successful-grasp
windows of $1.5$\,mm (\texttt{parallel\_jaw\_stock}) and $2.5$\,mm
(\texttt{parallel\_jaw\_TPU}), while all four finray variants spanned
the full $\geq 10$\,mm analysis window (Figure~\ref{fig:working-range-hist});
their ranges were right-censored at the window end, and the underlying
record extends beyond it.

\section{Working Range Under Tilt}
\label{sec:pj-vs-finray-results}

Extending the comparison to all five tested orientations
(Figure~\ref{fig:working-range-tilt}) narrowed the parallel-jaw windows
further, to $0.5$--$2.5$\,mm, with two stock-jaw tilt conditions
producing no successful grasp at any depth. Every finray variant
retained its full $\geq 10$\,mm window under every orientation tested.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{working_range_tilt.png}
\caption[Working range under tilt, all variants]{Successful-grasp
working range on the calibrated depth axis, all five tested orientations
per variant. \textcolor{red}{$\times$}: no successful attempt at any
depth. Hatched bars are right-censored at the analysis-window end.}
\label{fig:working-range-tilt}
\end{figure}

The parallel jaws auto-failed on the Franka excess-force safety monitor
at depths only $1$--$1.5$\,mm beyond their successful-grasp window; the
finrays never triggered the safety monitor within the tested $-2$ to
$+16$\,mm range on any variant.

\section{Tilt-Aligned Response Comparison}
\label{sec:tilt-similarity-results}

Plotting each finray variant's five tilt-condition curves together on
the calibrated depth axis (Figure~\ref{fig:tilt-similarity}) shows how
tightly the orientations track one another within a variant. The mean
spread across the five orientations, averaged over the calibrated
window, was $1.3$--$2.4$\,N depending on variant --- small relative to
the $30$--$51$\,N knee peaks reported in
Table~\ref{tab:zero-tilt-summary}, and consistent with the depth-gated
tilt robustness reported in Section~\ref{sec:bipolar-bias-results}: the
curves separate near the boundary and converge in the plateau.

\section{One Shared Curve Shape Across the Finray Family}
\label{sec:shape-results}
\label{sec:zero-tilt-results}

Under zero tilt, all four finray variants exhibited a stereotyped
\emph{bell-plus-valley} force-depth response with four regions:
(i)~a boundary regime where contact force rose steeply with press depth,
(ii)~a knee peak around $+1.5$ to $+3$\,mm past the detected surface,
(iii)~a valley trough near $+6$ to $+8$\,mm, and (iv)~a recovery regime
deeper than $+10$\,mm. Table~\ref{tab:zero-tilt-summary} summarises the
key features; the four features lay within $\pm 1$\,mm of the same
depth locations across all four variants, and the absolute force
magnitudes scaled approximately with the frontal contact area of the
finger.

\section{Bipolar Tilt Bias on the Finray Family}
\label{sec:bipolar-bias-results}

A $\pm 2.5^{\circ}$ tilt produced a signed bias
$b(d,a) = |F_z^{+2.5^{\circ}}(d,a)| - |F_z^{-2.5^{\circ}}(d,a)|$
whose depth trajectory itself followed a bipolar shape: a strong signed
peak at boundary depths, a zero-crossing near the knee, a sign-reversed
plateau through the valley, and depth-gated attenuation toward zero at
deep press. The clearest example was the y-axis bias on
\texttt{finray\_26\_5deg}, which had full coverage across 14 sign-flip
pairs from $-2$ to $+16$\,mm:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Boundary peak: $b = +5.40 \pm 0.12$\,N at $d = -1.5$\,mm.
  \item Zero crossing: between $d = -0.5$\,mm ($b = +2.00$\,N) and
        $d = +1.5$\,mm ($b = -1.35$\,N).
  \item Knee plateau: $b = -1.57 \pm 0.21$\,N at $d = +2.0$\,mm,
        persisting to $d = +3.0$\,mm.
  \item Depth-gated recovery: $|b| < 0.9$\,N at all depths beyond
        $d = +4$\,mm.
\end{itemize}

The x-axis bias on \texttt{finray\_26\_5deg} had a similar bipolar
shape but with opposite sign at the boundary
($b_x = -4.17 \pm 0.32$\,N at $d = -1.5$\,mm).

\label{sec:cross-axis-results}

the $5^{\circ}$ mount condition.

\section{Trial Outcome Distribution}
\label{sec:trial-outcomes}

Trial outcomes were classified into three categories: \emph{success}
(cloth held after lift), \emph{no-grasp} (trial completed with valid
$F_z$ measurement but cloth slipped at lift), and \emph{failed trial}
(controller aborted or trial did not complete a stationary window).
The failed-trial category was excluded from force-curve analyses;
no-grasp trials contributed valid force measurements. No-grasp fractions
at each variant's boundary depth are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:no-grasp-fraction}.
